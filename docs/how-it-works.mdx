# How it works

The evaluator answers one question: **is this a real problem worth solving, and am I the right person to solve it?**
You score 28 criteria once. The site then views those scores from several angles.

The tool is organised as a three-phase journey. Work through it in order. Each phase shows a progress bar, and the **Next →** button at the bottom of every step takes you to the following one.

| Phase | Question | Steps |
|---|---|---|
| **① Validate the idea** | Is this a real problem, in a market worth entering? | Scorecard · TAM/SAM/SOM · Five Forces · 3C |
| **② Design value & business model** | Who is it for, what value do you create, how does the business work? | Personas · Value Proposition Canvas · Business Model Canvas |
| **③ Craft the offer** | How do you package it so people feel stupid saying no? | Starving crowd · Value equation · Problems → solutions · Delivery · Trim & stack · Enhance · Your offer |

Don't polish an offer (phase 3) for an idea that failed validation (phase 1). The offer page warns you when the phase 1 verdict is weak.

## 1. Score from evidence, not optimism

Each criterion is scored **1 to 5**, and **5 is always the favourable end**. For "operational complexity", 5 means *simple to run*. For "customer acquisition cost", 5 means *cheap to acquire*.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 1 | Very weak. Evidence points against the idea. |
| 2 | Weak |
| 3 | Uncertain or average. You lean one way but cannot prove it. |
| 4 | Strong |
| 5 | Very strong. You have evidence: interviews, data, payments. |
| **?** | Unknown. Leave it unscored rather than guessing. |

Tick **evidence** next to a score when it is backed by something real: customer interviews, data, a pre-sale or a working prototype. The **Evidence-backed %** meter shows how much of your evaluation is fact rather than hunch.

:::tip
An honest "?" is more useful than an optimistic 4. Unknowns are not counted in any average, and they feed the **Validate next** list.
:::

## 2. Section scores and the overall score

- **Section score**: the average of the scored criteria in that section (A–E).
- **Overall score**: the average of the section scores. Each of the five areas counts equally, however many criteria it has.
- **Coverage**: how many of the 28 criteria you have scored.

## 3. Perspective lenses

The same scores are re-weighted to answer the question each stakeholder cares about. A lens score is a **weighted average of the scored criteria it uses**. Unscored items are skipped, and the lens shows how much of its weight is covered.

| Lens | The question it asks |
|---|---|
| 👤 Customer | Would I urgently want this, and is it clearly better than what I do today? |
| 💰 Investor | Is the market big enough, are the unit economics sound, and can it become large and defensible? |
| 🛠️ Founder / Operator | Can I build, sell and run this myself with the time, skills and cash I actually have? |
| ⚔️ Competitor / Market | If incumbents and copycats respond, does this still win and hold its position? |

## 4. Verdict

A verdict appears once at least 5 criteria are scored.

| Overall | Verdict |
|---|---|
| ≥ 4.0 | **Strong: pursue it** |
| 3.3 – 3.9 | **Promising: validate the risks** |
| 2.5 – 3.2 | **Weak: rework the idea** |
| < 2.5 | **Rethink or drop it** |

### Knockouts

Five criteria are marked **critical**: *problem severity, customer access, willingness to pay, technical feasibility* and *distribution strategy*. A single one scored **≤ 2** caps the verdict at **Weak**, whatever the average. A great product nobody pays for, or one you cannot reach customers for, does not average out.

The verdict is marked **provisional** when fewer than 60% of criteria are scored. A warning also appears when fewer than 40% of scores are evidence-backed.

## 5. Red flags and "Validate next"

- **Red flags**: every criterion scored 1 or 2, worst first.
- **Validate next**: up to six cheap experiments, ordered as follows:
  1. critical criteria scored low
  2. critical criteria still unknown
  3. other unknowns
  4. scores of 3 without evidence

Each comes with a concrete test, such as pre-selling for willingness to pay or a two-week spike for technical feasibility.

## 6. Market size: TAM · SAM · SOM

| Layer | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **TAM**: total addressable market | Everyone who could ever buy this. |
| **SAM**: serviceable available market | The part your product, geography and channels can actually serve. |
| **SOM**: serviceable obtainable market | What you can realistically win in 3–5 years. |

- **Bottom-up** (recommended): TAM = number of potential customers × annual revenue per customer. SAM is a % of TAM, and SOM is a % of SAM.
- **Top-down**: type in the three values from reports. Add a price if you want them converted into customer counts.
- Set a **revenue goal** and the tool checks it against your SOM:

| SOM ÷ goal | Suggested *Market size* score |
|---|---|
| ≥ 3× | 5 |
| ≥ 1.5× | 4 |
| ≥ 1× | 3 |
| ≥ 0.5× | 2 |
| below 0.5× | 1 |

  One click applies the suggestion to the scorecard.

The chart draws nested circles whose **areas** are proportional to the values. A log-scale bar chart sits beside it so a tiny SOM stays readable.

## 7. Porter's Five Forces

Michael Porter's model asks how attractive the **industry** is, separately from your product. Rate each force by **intensity**: 1 = weak (good for you), 5 = strong (squeezes profits).

| Force | Strong when… |
|---|---|
| ⚔️ Competitive rivalry | many similar players, slow growth, little differentiation |
| 🚪 Threat of new entrants | it is cheap to start and there are no network effects, brand or regulation barriers |
| 🧑‍💼 Buyer power | customers are few, price-sensitive, or can switch or build it themselves |
| 🏭 Supplier power | you depend on one platform, API or marketplace |
| 🔄 Threat of substitutes | manual work, spreadsheets or "do nothing" are good enough |

- **Industry attractiveness** = average of (6 − intensity), on the same 1–5 scale as everything else.
- The scorecard **suggests** an intensity for each force from related criteria. For example, rivalry is suggested from *competitive alternatives* and *differentiation*. One click fills any unrated forces.

## 8. 3C model

Kenichi Ohmae's 3C model says a strategy lives where three things overlap: **Customers** (what they need), **Company** (what you do well) and **Competitors** (where they fall short).

Each C gets a score from the related scorecard criteria, plus your own written analysis:
- **Customers:** severity, frequency, urgency, ICP, market size, access, willingness to pay.
- **Company:** founder-market fit, feasibility, distribution, validation speed, operations, margin, cash.
- **Competitors:** alternatives, differentiation, defensibility, switching costs. A high score here means your competitive position is strong.

The overlap is only as strong as the weakest circle.

## 9. User personas

A persona is a realistic profile of one key customer type. Build it from interviews, not imagination.

- **Profile:**
  - basics: name, role, age, location, budget
  - a real quote from an interview
  - background
  - role in the purchase (user, buyer, decision maker, influencer, gatekeeper)
- **Lists:** goals, frustrations, jobs to be done, tools and behaviours, where to reach them.
- **Behaviour sliders:**
  - price-sensitive ↔ value-focused
  - late ↔ early adopter
  - do-it-yourself ↔ done-for-you
  - tech-savviness
  - risk appetite
- **Evidence:** record how many interviews the persona is based on. With zero, it is flagged as *assumption-based*.
- **Send to Value Proposition Canvas:** makes the persona the canvas segment. Its jobs, frustrations and goals become canvas jobs, pains and gains.

## 10. Value Proposition Canvas

Based on the canvas in *Value Proposition Design* (Osterwalder, Pigneur, Bernarda & Smith, 2014). The canvas has two sides.

**Customer profile** (circle) describes one segment:

| Block | Rated by | Types |
|---|---|---|
| 🧰 Jobs | importance | functional · social · emotional · supporting |
| 😣 Pains | severity | undesired outcome · obstacle · risk |
| 🌟 Gains | relevance | required · expected · desired · unexpected |

**Value map** (square) describes your offer:
- products & services
- **pain relievers**, each linked to the pains it addresses
- **gain creators**, each linked to the gains it produces

**Fit** = the share of total pain-severity and gain-relevance weight that your relievers and creators address. Unaddressed items show in red on the canvas and the **fit matrix**. Click matrix cells to link or unlink.

| Fit | Suggested *Value proposition* score |
|---|---|
| ≥ 80% | 5 |
| ≥ 60% | 4 |
| ≥ 40% | 3 |
| ≥ 20% | 2 |
| below 20% | 1 |

The canvas tab also warns about:
- relievers or creators linked to nothing
- only functional jobs listed
- extreme pains left unrelieved
- more than 10 relievers and creators combined (trying to do too much)

Extra tools on the same tab:
- **Ad-lib statements:** "Our ___ help ___ who want to ___ by ___ and ___ (unlike ___)". These are pre-filled from your top-ranked items. Star the best one and use it as the idea's pitch.
- **10 characteristics of a great value proposition:** a self-check out of 10.
- **Path to fit:** problem–solution → product–market → business-model fit, with your evidence for each.
- **Test cards:**
  - Each card has four parts: *we believe… / to verify, we will… / and measure… / we are right if…*
  - Each card has a status and a "what we learned" field.
  - Cards can be suggested from the biggest gaps in your canvas.

## 11. Business Model Canvas

The nine building blocks from *Business Model Generation* (Osterwalder & Pigneur, 2010), laid out in the classic grid:

| Left: efficiency | Centre | Right: value |
|---|---|---|
| 🤝 Key partners · ⚙️ Key activities · 🧱 Key resources · 💸 Cost structure | 🎁 Value propositions | 💬 Customer relationships · 📣 Channels · 👥 Customer segments · 💰 Revenue streams |

**Sticky notes:**
- Add notes to any block and colour-code them. A good habit is one colour per customer segment.
- Notes start as dashed **assumptions**. Mark them **✓ validated** once you have evidence. The tab tracks the % validated.
- Revenue and cost notes take a type (subscription, usage, commission… or fixed and variable) and a **monthly amount**. The tab then shows revenue against costs, profit or loss, margin, and fixed costs.

**✨ Pre-fill from other tabs** imports, without duplicates:
- personas → customer segments
- your starred value proposition sentence → value propositions
- persona channels → channels
- canvas products → key resources
- the TAM price → revenue streams

**Warnings** flag:
- empty blocks
- several segments sharing a single value proposition
- a model that is mostly untested assumptions
- costs higher than revenue

## 12. Offer builder (phase 3)

Based on the method in Alex Hormozi's *$100M Offers* (2021). Everything is paraphrased.

1. **Starving crowd.** Rate the market 1–10 on four indicators (/40):
   - massive pain
   - purchasing power
   - easy to target
   - growing

   Pick a core market (health, wealth or relationships) and niche down. Enter your price against competitors' prices. The point is to charge what it's worth, not to compete on price.
2. **Value equation.** *Value = (dream outcome × perceived likelihood) ÷ (time delay × effort & sacrifice).*
   - Rate each driver 1–10. The score is shown on a 0–10 log scale.
   - The tool names your biggest lever and gives a tip for improving it.
3. **Problems → solutions.**
   - List every obstacle before, during and after the purchase. Import them from your canvas pains and persona frustrations.
   - Tag each with the doubt it represents: not worth it, won't work for me, takes too long, too hard.
   - Flip each into a "How to…" solution. ✨ suggests a first draft.
4. **Delivery vehicles.**
   - For each solution, brainstorm ways to deliver it, varying three things:
     - personal attention: 1-on-1, small group, one-to-many
     - customer effort: DIY, done with you, done for you
     - medium
   - Cheat codes include the 10× and 1/10th price tests.
5. **Trim & stack.**
   - Rate every delivery idea on value to the customer and cost to you. A matrix sorts them into *keep / keep if profitable / optional / cut*.
   - Give the kept items a perceived worth and stack them. The tool shows total value against price, aiming for at least 10×.
6. **Enhance.**
   - **Scarcity:** limited seats, limited bonuses, or a never-again offer.
   - **Urgency:** rolling cohorts, seasonal promotions, price or bonus deadlines, or an exploding opportunity.
   - **Bonuses:** each one should kill a specific doubt. Tools beat training. Bonus value should exceed the core.
   - **Guarantee:** unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee or implied, written as "if X, we will Y", with a vivid name.
   - **MAGIC name:** Magnetic reason why · Avatar · Goal · Interval · Container word.
7. **Your offer.** A one-page offer and a 12-point strength checklist. Each open item has a "fix →" link to the right step. Copy the offer as plain text for a sales page or DM.

| Checks passed | Label |
|---|---|
| 11–12 | Grand Slam Offer 🏆 |
| 8–10 | Strong offer |
| 5–7 | Decent offer |
| 0–4 | Commodity offer |

## 13. Report tab

The **Report** tab shows every result full-width with large charts: both radars, persona cards, the Value Proposition Canvas, the Business Model Canvas, the offer one-pager, the Five Forces diagram, the 3C diagram, the market chart, red flags and next tests. Use **Print / PDF** from that tab for a one-document summary.

## 14. Privacy, saving and sharing

- Everything is stored **only in your browser** (localStorage). There is no server, account or tracking. The only exception is when you click **Save to Google Drive**, which sends the idea from your browser to your own Drive.
- **Share link**: the whole idea is packed into the link itself (after the `#`). Whoever opens it gets their own copy.
- **Backup JSON** saves all your ideas to a file. **Import JSON** restores them on any device.
- **☁️ Save to Google Drive** signs you in with Google and writes the idea to an *Idea Evaluator* folder in your Drive: a Google Doc with the full report and a `.json` backup you can import later. Saving again updates the same files. The app can only see files it created there.
- **Print / PDF** prints a clean report. **Markdown** export works well for Notion, GitHub or docs.
